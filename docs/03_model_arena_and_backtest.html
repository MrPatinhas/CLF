📖 Field Guide: What Does Every Metric in This Leaderboard Actually Mean?
  • MAE (Mean Absolute Error, e.g., 2.65 pts): Exactly what your intuition says—the straight arithmetic average of absolute errors $|y - \hat{y}|$. On any random player in a round, the prediction is off by $\pm 2.65$ points on average. Every point of error is weighted equally ($1\times$).
  • RMSE (Root Mean Squared Error, e.g., 3.51 pts): Squares the errors $(y - \hat{y})^2$ before averaging, then takes the square root so it returns to Cartola point units. Because it squares errors first, a single 10-point miss (e.g., predicting $4.0$ when a player explodes for $14.0$) counts as $10^2 = 100$ squared units—the same as one hundred 1-point misses! That is why RMSE (3.51) > MAE (2.65): RMSE heavily penalizes missing big "Mito" spikes or red-card disasters.
  • R² (Explained Variance, e.g., 0.1026 = 10.3%): Measures the fraction of single-match variance explained compared to guessing the global average every week ($R^2 = 1 - \text{SSE}_{\text{model}} / \text{SSE}_{\text{mean}}$). Why is $10.3\%$ actually huge in single-match fantasy soccer? Because ~85–90% of single-game Cartola variance is irreducible in-match Poisson randomness (a ball hitting the post vs. going in is a $+7.2$ pt swing; a $90'$ opponent goal wipes out $-5.0$ SG points). Negative $R^2$ (like Naive_Roll5 = -0.038) means a noisy 5-game average is actually worse than predicting the mean!
  • How We Compute PR-AUC on a Regression Model (Threshold-Free Ranking!): You asked a great question—"PR-AUC is a classification metric, so how does it work on a regression prediction?"
    1. First, we define the binary ground-truth event $Y_{\text{mito}} \in \{0, 1\}$ (either Fixed: actual score $\ge 8.0$ pts, or Quantile: actual score in the Top 15% of that position in that round).
    2. Second, we use the regression model's continuous predicted score $\hat{y}_i$ (e.g. $6.4$ pts, $5.1$ pts, $3.2$ pts) as the ranking score!
    3. PR-AUC sweeps a cutoff threshold $c$ from highest predicted score down to lowest, plotting Precision (of the players we predicted above $c$, what % actually "mitaram"?) vs. Recall (of all true "Mitos", what % did we catch above $c$?). A random guesser gets a PR-AUC equal to the base rate (~0.13–0.15); our ensemble reaches 0.2429, meaning the top of our predicted list is nearly $2\times$ as dense with true Mitos!
  • Mean Round Spearman $\rho$ (e.g., 0.3287): Measures pure rank order inside each round ($[-1, +1]$). In Cartola, if a rainy round depresses everyone's score by $-1.5$ points, MAE/RMSE punish you, even if you still drafted the exact best 11 players! Spearman $\rho$ checks how well the model's 1st, 2nd, 3rd... ranked players line up with the actual 1st, 2nd, 3rd... scorers of that round.
  • Elite Star Bias (Pred − Actual on Top 8% Players): Measures whether the model anchors geniuses (Arrascaeta, Hulk, Estêvão) toward the positional mean. Negative means under-predicting stars; Residual_XGBoost (-0.085) virtually eliminates this anchoring!

💊 Learnable Pill: Why Residual Target Learning (`y - EB_Baseline`) Solves Tree Regressive Bias
Your initial intuition was 100% validated by the data:
  • On the top 8% of players (stars like Arrascaeta, Hulk, Estêvão, Pedro, Raphael Veiga), standard Positional_LightGBM exhibits an Elite Outlier Bias of -0.624 pts (and Market_Price has -1.004 pts) because tree leaf regularization shrinks extreme leaves back toward the positional mean (~3.1 pts).
  • When we instead train Residual_XGBoost to predict $\Delta y_{i,t} = y_{i,t} - \hat{\mu}_{\text{dual\_EB}, i, t-1}$ and add the player's Dual-Memory Empirical Bayes baseline back, the Elite Outlier Bias drops to -0.085 pts (virtually zero bias!).

4. Positional Predictability Breakdown: Why Coach (`TEC`) is the Most Predictable Position!

Comparing Spearman rank correlation and Top-15% Quantile PR-AUC across all 6 positions.

💊 Learnable Pill: Law of Large Numbers on the Pitch (`TEC` vs `GOL`)
In your prompt, you wondered if a Coach (TEC) model would be a "horoscope". Look at the chart above: Coach (TEC) actually has the HIGHEST Spearman Rank Correlation ($ ho = 0.43$) and HIGHEST PR-AUC ($0.33$) of any position in Cartola! Why? Because a single striker's score is a noisy sample of $N=1$ player, whereas the Coach's score is the arithmetic average of $N=11$ players. By the Law of Large Numbers, idiosyncratic individual variance cancels out at the team level ($\sigma_{\bar{X}} = \sigma / \sqrt{11}$), leaving pure team-vs-opponent expected value! Meanwhile, Goalkeepers (GOL) are the hardest to rank ($ ho \approx 0.14$) because a keeper faces a paradox: weak defenses concede goals (losing $+5.0$ SG), while strong defenses face zero shots (earning zero $+1.3$ DE saves).
